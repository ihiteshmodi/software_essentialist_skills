## Summary
Khalil introduces the 12 essential principles as foundational "thinking tools" that piece together the four pillars in a manageable way, closer to actual practice. A woodworker parable shows tools/techniques without fundamentals fail. He distinguishes two kinds of knowledge (Sophia vs Phronesis) and says the principles were distilled from studying masterful (phronimos) developers. The essentials fall into three categories: metaphysical (abstraction, feedback loop), theoretical (pre-code: value identification, domain discovery, ...), and physical (code: composition, vertical slicing, ...). This section is "planting seeds"; the essentials are refined throughout the phases of craftship.

## Core ideas
- **Woodworker parable**: focused on tools (plane, drill [transcript: "he could solve the drill"]) but not fundamentals (right types of wood); couldn't build a [mirror table] when needed.
- Tools, techniques, patterns matter, but **principles** — "thinking tools upon which you can orient yourself" — are foundational.
- Why we need the 12 essentials: the four pillars are hard to piece together; need to go deeper, closer to actual practice.
- **Two types of knowledge**: **Sophia** (love of knowledge / philosophy) and **Phronesis** (wisdom / mastery). Principles derived from studying books and "phronimos" developers [transcript: "phronimus"] — the masterful ones.
- **Three categories of essentials**:
  1. **Metaphysical** — abstraction and the feedback loop. Based on core mental models, especially systems thinking ("the world is a patchwork of systems that are input-output systems to each other"). "Basically the way the world works."
  2. **Theoretical** — everything before writing code: planning; who we're helping, how, capabilities, features, use cases, concrete examples. Examples: value identification, domain discovery.
  3. **Physical** — actually writing source code and implementing features. Examples: composition, vertical slicing.
- What to focus on now: plant seeds; let them frame how you look at code and judge whether you're on the right or wrong path. Refined over time in the **phases of craftship**.
