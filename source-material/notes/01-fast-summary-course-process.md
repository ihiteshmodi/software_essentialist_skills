## Summary
Top-to-bottom overview of the whole course so the learner knows what is coming and can plan. The core problem addressed is developers' lack of full confidence in their own design, architecture, testing and strategy decisions. The central skill to develop is **problem decomposition**, supported by metacognition and solid mental models. The course is organized as: The Metaphysics (mental models + 12 essentials + skills) -> Phases of Craftship (5 phases, apprenticeship with assignments) -> optional Final Project -> The Guess Points (catalog of decomposition steps) -> wrap-up applying the models to life.

## Core ideas
- Main pain: a nagging "am I doing things right?" feeling; lack of full confidence in design/architecture/testing/strategy decisions.
- Requirements and challenges constantly change and are dynamic -> they require bespoke approaches, so memorized recipes are insufficient.
- **The main skill = problem decomposition.**
- Great programmers, like philosophers, have **metacognition** — awareness of your own thinking: knowing how you solve problems, how you think about solving them, and being able to refine that.
- Goal: develop solid mental models to consistently approach work -> ability to solve almost any problem consistently.
- The **12 essentials** give a strong baseline in what actually matters: **testing, design, architecture, strategy**.
- Key tools named: **systems thinking**, **the guess points** (how to break apart / decompose problems), **FA²STR** (a design method to convert a problem into systematic action steps), plus design and testing strategies.
- Applies to any side of the stack (front end, back end, desktop), new and existing projects — "because it's all just about problem decomposition."
- Course is centered on mental models, not topics -> touches psychology, philosophy, strategy, subjectivity. Skill develops through practice and "filling in the blanks."
- Metaphysics is taught first on purpose: "plant seeds" so that in the Phases of Craftship the learner already knows the mental models / 12 essentials and can put them into practice.
- Foundational questions rarely asked but crucial: What is software? Why do we write software? Who are we writing it for? What is scalability? How do you get scalable code? What's stopping you from getting scalable code? Without clarity on these, achieving them isn't possible.
- The 12 essential principles = "the 80/20" of what Khalil learned studying the greats, books and pioneers.
- Responsibility-driven design / the responsibility-first phase = "the most granular level we can use to think about code."

## Named frameworks / mental models
- **Course structure (5 parts)**
  1. **The Metaphysics** — general mental models; foundational questions; the 12 essential principles; skills (ideal developer workflow, FA²STR).
  2. **The Phases of Craftship** — apprenticeship with lessons + assignments; develop and refine the 12 essentials across 5 phases.
  3. **Optional Final Project** — build something that "gives back time / enriches time" / is valuable; solo or community-based (pair with other devs).
  4. **The Guess Points** — a catalog of problem-decomposition steps to return to for further study.
  5. **Wrap-up** — apply the mental models to code and to life; "try out your next path."
- **The 5 Phases of Craftship** (purpose: staged path from building to crafting; ~6–7 years of Khalil's study/practice reverse-engineered)
  1. **Code-First** — learn to be full stack.
  2. **Best-Practice-First** — introduce many of the 12 essentials by refactoring what was built in code-first.
  3. **Pattern-First** — front-end and back-end patterns and how to apply them.
  4. **Responsibility-First** — design bespoke solutions; actual object-oriented design; responsibility-driven design (RDD). "Where a lot of developers get stuck"; "not often traveled."
  5. **Value-First** — now able to do problem decomposition; thinking about what we want to do and strategic ways to work; use tools (guess points, FA²STR) to decompose problems in different contexts.
- **FA²STR** — design method to convert a problem into action steps; decomposes into problem steps.
- **The Guess Points** — catalog of individual parts of the problem decomposition process.
- **The Ideal Developer Workflow** — named skill in the metaphysics section (details later).
- **Systems thinking** — named key mental model (details later).

## Definitions
- **Problem decomposition** — "the main skill"; breaking a problem into steps you can act on.
- **Metacognition** — awareness of your own thinking; knowing how you solve problems and refining it.
- **Code-first developer** — someone who "might not know how to craft but you can build."
- **The Metaphysics** — section where all mental models are introduced; clarity on foundational ideas aligned with reality.
- **Guess points** — catalog of problem-decomposition steps.
- **12 essentials** — 80/20 of testing, design, architecture, strategy.

## Rules & heuristics
- Get the big picture first so you can plan and know what to expect.
- Align your understanding of software with reality before trying to write scalable code.
- Spend significant time practicing assignments; use the community when stuck.
- Prerequisites: comfortable building on the front end, or already a back-end dev; professional experience helps (more reference experiences / pain -> deeper appreciation); know how to learn new tech and debug technical snags (little time spent debugging in course).

## Anti-patterns / mistakes called out
- Topic-focused learning without developing the thinking skill (implied contrast with "other programming courses").
- Not asking foundational questions (what is software, why, for whom, what is scalability).

## AI-era relevance hint
- Problem decomposition is exactly what an engineer must supply to AI agents: breaking work into verifiable, well-scoped steps.
- Metacognition lets you evaluate whether agent-generated designs follow sound reasoning rather than just "working."
- Foundational questions (why/for whom/what is value) are what AI can't answer for you — they define the spec.
