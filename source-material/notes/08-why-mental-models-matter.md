## Summary
Words like "dog" or "red" instantly trigger internal representations — and developers constantly create internal representations for themselves and others through code, making every developer an **abstraction designer**. Metaphysics is the branch of philosophy that defines reality and our understanding of it (cause and effect). Mental models are simplifications of the world held mostly in the subconscious; **a mental model is only as good as how closely it maps to reality**. Khalil's own flawed models (API-first, database-first) produced expensive, unscalable messes. Good models need first-principles clarity and let you act well at quantum moments like the start of a project.

## Core ideas
- Prompt "dog" / "red" -> something pops up automatically = an **internal representation**.
- Much of development = creating internal representations for yourself and other developers reading your code -> **you are an abstraction designer**, creating and using mental models.
- **Metaphysics** = branch of philosophy that helps define reality and our understanding of it; essentially cause and effect ("kick the ball, it moves").
- The brain loves to make sense of things; doesn't handle uncertainty well.
- The subconscious (~90% of the mind) holds "maps of meaning" / mental models; they are simplifications that let us avoid re-thinking things.
- Take ownership of how your subconscious is programmed — you didn't consciously choose what "dog" evoked.
- Bad models -> missing outcomes: graphic designer doing only their own style, no one buys; coder only having fun/trying new tech, no one hires -> something missing in the model.
- **A mental model is only as good as how close it maps to reality.** Good model = yields beneficial outcomes; lets you think through where you are and where you want to be.
- Most learners' goal: write scalable code — Khalil couldn't for a long time because his models weren't based in reality.
- **Flawed model 1 — API-first:** "build this REST endpoint, that REST endpoint, connect front end and back end." Worked for a while, then led to expensive messes; "reality is not based on APIs or RESTful endpoints"; reality is more nuanced and complex.
- **Flawed model 2 — Database/data-model-first:** start with data and relationships from a code level. Also led to non-scalable code.
- These conceptual levels map to the **Phases of Craftship**: code-first -> best practices -> patterns -> responsibility-first -> value (most valuable).
- Good mental models are based in reality and require **first-principles clarity**.
- First-principles clarity gives access to **quantum moments** — moments more important than others where right action yields good results. Example: **the start of a project** — code-first thinking designs from the database first (undesirable path); value-based consciousness, with clarity on how reality works and how software is intertwined in it, uses other techniques that lead toward scalable code.
- Key: ask essential questions to dig into the "database" of your subconscious and reprogram/reorient models about software development, scalable code, your role, and how to do it well.
- Normally takes a long time to learn in practice; Khalil used the essentialism process (consume content, practice, talk to experts, see what works) and shares the resulting representations.

## Named frameworks / mental models
- **Mental model quality = fidelity to reality** — purpose: evaluate any model; test: does it produce beneficial outcomes and let you navigate from current to desired state?
- **Developer as abstraction designer** — purpose: recognize code as shared internal representations for other developers.
- **Phases of Craftship as levels of thinking** — Code-First (thinking about code) -> Best-Practice-First -> Pattern-First -> Responsibility-First -> Value-First.
- **Quantum moments** — high-leverage decision points (e.g., project start).
- **Essential questions** — purpose: surface and reprogram subconscious mental models.

## Definitions
- **Metaphysics** — branch of philosophy that helps us define reality and our understanding of it; cause and effect.
- **Mental model** — a simplification of the world (map of meaning) held mostly subconsciously that helps interpret reality and decide future action without rethinking.
- **Internal representation** — what arises in the mind when encountering a word/concept; what code creates for its readers.
- **Quantum moment** — a moment in time more important than others where correct action yields good results.

## Rules & heuristics
- Judge a model by how closely it maps to reality and the outcomes it produces.
- Don't start projects from the API or the database; start from a value-based understanding of reality (use cases/value — see Lesson 5) (partly inferred).
- Seek first-principles clarity on foundational questions: what software is, your role, how to do it well.
- Be aware your code creates mental models for others.

## Anti-patterns / mistakes called out
- **API-first/REST-endpoint-first** development as the fundamental view of software -> expensive messes.
- **Database/data-model-first** design -> unscalable code.
- Coding for fun / trying new tech without mapping to what's valued (why no one hires).
- Letting unexamined subconscious models drive decisions.
